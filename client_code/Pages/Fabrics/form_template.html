Regain is how much water vapour a fibre holds at standard conditions, as a share of its dry weight: high regain feels cool and absorbs sweat; low regain dries fast but feels clammy. The burn test: hold a small snippet in tweezers over a sink, away from anything flammable. Blends give mixed results; it tells you the family, not the exact fibre.

In the weave drawings, green squares are where the lengthwise warp thread is on top; brass is the crosswise weft.

The iron: ISO 3758 dots

Blends: use the setting of the most delicate fibre in them. A crossed-out iron means don't iron at all.

Pressing like a tailor

  • Press, don't iron: lift and lower; sliding stretches seams.
  • Press every seam flat as sewn, then open, before it is crossed by another.
  • Use a press cloth (silk organza is see-through and takes the heat) on wool, dark colours and anything that can shine.
  • Curves go over a tailor's ham; sleeves over a sleeve board; wool gets steam then a clapper, held until cool.
  • Velvet and corduroy: steam from the back over a towel or needle board.

Wool "Super" numbers

The number is a fineness grade set by the wool trade (IWTO, ISO 18103) and written into US labelling law: each step of 10 is half a micron finer. Finer is softer and more delicate; Super 100s–130s suit everyday suits.
